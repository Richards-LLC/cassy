tantivy wins above 200 k documents; sqlite-fts wins below it — the crossover is the decision

Three full-text backends on the same corpus at four sizes, ten iterations each after warm-up. Reference candidate: sqlite-fts (current). Source of truth: 2026-08-20-fts-backend-benchmark.md · concept brief

Four metrics, one x axis, one crossover

Winner: tantivy above 200 k documents, by 3.9× on p99 latency at 400 k (29 ms vs 112 ms) and 2.2× on build time, at the cost of 31 % more resident memory. Below 200 k, sqlite-fts remains faster at p50 and cheaper in memory, and nothing justifies a migration.

Where the loser wins

sqlite-fts stays ahead at p50 up to 100 k documents (2.9 ms vs 3.6 ms) and uses 24–35 % less memory at every size. A deployment that never exceeds 150 k documents and runs on a 1 GB instance should keep it. pg-trgm never wins a column, but it needs no separate process, which this benchmark does not price.

Not measured: concurrent writers, snapshot restore time, and query-language differences (tantivy has no NEAR). Each could move the decision for a specific product; none changes the crossover.
