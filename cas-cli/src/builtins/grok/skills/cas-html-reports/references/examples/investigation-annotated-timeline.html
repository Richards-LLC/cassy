Nightly index rebuilds began timing out the night the batch size went to 5 000 — and stopped the night it went back

Question: why did the nightly index rebuild start exceeding its 30-minute timeout after 2026-07-29? Source of truth: 2026-08-07-index-rebuild-timeouts.md · concept brief: .concept.md

Seventeen nights on one axis

The 2026-07-29 change of INDEX_BATCH_SIZE from 500 to 5 000 (a1b2c3d) caused the timeouts: rebuild time rose from 16–19 minutes to 38–47 minutes the same night and returned to 18 minutes the night the value was reverted.

Confidence: high. One variable changed, the effect appeared and disappeared with it, and the mechanism (Postgres statement memory spill above 4 000 rows per batch) reproduces locally.

The corpus grew 2.1 % over the window (1.94 M → 1.98 M documents). Growth of that size cannot account for a 2.3× duration change; it was ruled out by re-running the 07-28 snapshot at batch 5 000, which took 40 min.

Reasoning chain

  1. Duration was flat for a week, then more than doubled on the night of one deploy that touched only INDEX_BATCH_SIZE.
  2. Two later deploys that did not touch indexing left the duration unchanged; the corpus grew 2.1 %. Neither explains a 2.3× change.
  3. Postgres logs from 07-29 onward show temporary file: size 1.4 GB on every INSERT … ON CONFLICT batch; at batch 500 no temporary files appear. The spill is the mechanism.
  4. Reverting the batch size on 08-06 returned the duration to 18 min the same night. Cause established by insertion and removal.

Ruled out: corpus growth (re-run of the 07-28 snapshot), the 07-31 connection-pool change (reverted in a local run with no effect), and disk pressure (free space never dropped below 61 %).

What would falsify this

A rebuild at batch 500 that exceeds 30 minutes on a corpus under 2.1 M documents, or a rebuild at batch 5 000 that completes under 20 minutes with work_mem unchanged. Neither has been observed.
