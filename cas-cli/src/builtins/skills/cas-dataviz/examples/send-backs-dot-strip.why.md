# Why it works — send-backs-dot-strip.html

Reader's task: see how a failure rate is distributed across a small population.

- **Claim-title.** "Three of nineteen deliveries carried every send-back; sixteen merged clean" is the
  finding. "Send-backs by task" would only have named an axis.
- **Form and why.** A dot strip (one mark per delivery, rings for send-backs) instead of a bar or a
  rate. The population is 19; every unit can be shown, and showing them reveals the clustering that a
  "26 %" would hide. The rings are concentric so a delivery's send-back count is a shape, not a color.
- **Annotation on the mark.** The connector joins the three ringed dots to one sentence that carries
  the numbers; the reader never leaves the figure to find the point.
- **Color last.** `--evidence` for the population, `--verdict` for the marks the claim rests on;
  nothing else is colored. It survives grayscale print because the rings are strokes.
- **Twin table and provenance.** Exact rows and the definition of "send-back" sit under the figure.
- **Verified.** Rendered headless at desktop and 390×844 in light and dark; annotation text fits the
  viewBox at both widths.

accessibility 4.
