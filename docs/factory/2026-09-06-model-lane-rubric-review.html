Recommendations, in order of evidence

  1. Heavy: Sol/high primary, Luna/xhigh first fallback, Astra/high explicit-only until five measured deliveries. Sol 8% send-backs on 12 at $8.80; Luna 9% on 173 at $0.51; Astra 0 deliveries at 2.5× Sol's price. Astra's published edge (TB 4.0 57.9 vs 37.3; AA "Pareto frontier of token efficiency") is the reason to measure it: run the next five heavy tasks as Astra/high and Sol/high pairs on the same brief and compare send-backs, tool calls and output tokens from the rollouts.
  2. Supervisor: measure Fable medium before deciding, and make the registry say what runs. Every supervisor session ran high because Claude Code defaults to high and the spawn path never sets effortLevel. A supervisor day is $132–$284 at list, 13–28× the workers it reviews. Experiment: two consecutive supervisor days at medium scored by the 3.17.2 actionable-idle metric and send-back counts. Until then set supervisor/taste default_effort = "high".
  3. Cut the supervisor's cache-write bill. 53% of the 09-05 supervisor cost is 1-hour-TTL cache writes ($20/M on Fable 5.1, 80× the read price). A harness setting, not a model choice; a 5-minute TTL is worth a one-day trial with the same metric.
  4. Light: give Haiku a thinking budget or delete the lane. Haiku has no effort parameter; its one coding score (73.3%) needs a 128K thinking budget. Encode thinking_budget in the recipe with marker-tested chores, or route light at Luna/xhigh — what happens today, at $0.51.
  5. Track output tokens and tool calls per delivery per lane, weekly, from the rollouts. They are in every rollout and are what vendors will not publish per effort. Luna's p50→p90 output spread (45K→74K) and Opus's 102K/78-call profile are the first local intelligence-vs-efficiency signal; send-back rate alone cannot separate "cheap and sloppy" from "expensive and careful".

What the data cannot answer: Sol vs Luna vs Astra on identical tasks (rec. 1), Fable medium vs high on identical supervision (rec. 2), and any tool-calling score for the OpenAI or Anthropic models (no τ²/BFCL figures published).

Where the rubric fails

1 · Heavy is routed on reputation against the data

Astra has zero worker deliveries in the window and one recorded coordination stall. Sol has one delivery and one send-back. Luna has nineteen merged deliveries and is not in heavy at all.

Heavy is the lane for safety-critical implementation, where "quietly stops" is the worst failure mode.

2 · Silent fallback to the replaced default

Opus 5/high was the built-in supervisor default until 22:00Z on 2026-09-05. It is now the automatic backup for both judgment lanes. A bad auth hour changes the coordinator's behaviour with nobody deciding it.

cas-255e adds a loud receipt. Loud is not approved.

3 · Effort has no stated rationale

Luna xhigh only; Fable medium; Astra medium by recipe but high in heavy; Sol high; Haiku low. A reader cannot tell cost decisions from quality decisions.

4 · Nothing is measured

Every routing change this week came from anecdote: Astra→Fable taste, Fable supervisor, Opus fallbacks, Astra heavy. The actionable-idle metric in 3.17.2 is the first number the rubric has ever had.

5 · The light lane is decorative

Zero uses in the window. Every mechanical chore went to Luna because Haiku is not trusted with builtin marker tests.

6 · Cross-harness fallback inside a lane

Standard falls back from Codex to Claude: different hooks, skill mirror, and account, mid-epic.

7 · Fallback edges declared but disabled

Until cas-255e lands, taste and supervisor carry a fallback that never fires while the comment says "fail closed". A registry that documents one policy and executes another is a review hazard.

Optimizations, in order of leverage

  1. Measure three numbers per lane, per week from data CAS already records: send-backs per delivery (request_changes notes), actionable-idle minutes (3.17.2 metric), assignment-to-first-push minutes (lease start vs first pushed tip). Print them in the generated route table so the rubric and its scorecard are read together.
  2. Promote by trial, not by directive. Keep Sol as heavy primary; make Astra/high the heavy fallback for two weeks; promote only if its send-back rate is at or below Sol's on at least five deliveries.
  3. Make fallbacks explicit decisions. A lane fallback fires only after the supervisor is told the primary is unavailable and the receipt names both recipes. For the supervisor lane, prefer fail-closed plus an operator alert over a silent model change.
  4. Add an effort column with a reason. One sentence per lane: why this effort, what it costs relative to the lane below, and what evidence would change it.
  5. Retire or re-scope light. Route it at Luna/xhigh for tiny chores (what happens today anyway) or give Haiku a bounded class of work with its own marker tests.
  6. Keep fallbacks inside a harness. Standard should fall back to another Codex recipe or fail closed; cross-harness fallbacks belong to the operator, not the registry.
  7. Give Luna a seat in heavy. On this window's evidence it is the safest implementation lane we have; at minimum it should be the heavy fallback ahead of Sol.
