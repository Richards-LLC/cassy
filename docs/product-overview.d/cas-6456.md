## Core Concepts

A release train has shared publication authority in append-only Git history at `refs/cas/train-owner/<train>`. New trains publish their target and initial authority together with an atomic, expected-absent push. Existing targets require explicit enrollment by their current owner. The record binds the repository, owner process, generation, phase and exact target SHA. Supported target and tag publishers advance authority atomically with exact ref leases. Generic branch publication, worker spawn and child-close advancement use the owner helper or hold; missing authority never restores publication rights. Cleanup preserves enrolled train refs and their history, including merged trains.

Task filing and release tracking can prepare a local successor while its publisher is unavailable. They record the work and the publication hold without granting authority, so a missing owner cannot block cut receipts or lose follow-up tasks. The pending train cannot publish or admit workers until its registered owner initializes shared authority. Ordinary epic preparation uses only the matching branch's metadata and reuses its origin observation; explicitly unenrolled legacy trains retain their existing repair behavior.

`cas factory train owner-transfer <train> prepare|quiesce|accept|abort` and MCP `factory action=train_owner_transfer` share the transfer handler. Prepare names an immutable successor and closes new admissions by moving to draining. Accept requires positive quiescence and fresh provider checks before issuing a new generation and repairing the task projection. Earlier queued work stays held until the new owner explicitly admits it. An authenticated same-owner restart preserves its generation and outstanding reservations. Stale heartbeats and caller-supplied owner fields grant no authority.

PR auto-merge and Release API calls reserve their operation before making a request. Reservations bind the generation, launch process, source, PR, workflow blobs, allowed tag and actions. A provider-verified run and attempt may claim and finish its enumerated tag/Release continuations while draining; it cannot admit a new operation. Transfer waits for the whole release workflow, including its local reusable install-proof jobs, to finish and reconcile actual tag/Release outcomes. An empty queue cannot settle a request whose completion is unknown.

The publication CLI provides `cas factory train publication disarm-pr --train <train> --operation <id>` for the draining owner after its original PR-arm process recorded a successful response. It disables auto-merge and verifies the PR is unmerged and unqueued, with no associated publication outcome or active repository run. `reconcile-unpublished` records that same verified disposition after an independently completed disarm. `reconcile --train <train> --operation <id> --run <id> --attempt <n>` settles a claimed completed release chain. Operation ids are lookup keys in authority history, not credentials. Timeouts, missing metadata and unfinished API requests remain held across restarts.

The approved guarantee covers cooperating enrolled publishers. Raw Git/API credential holders and unsupported binaries can bypass a client fence. Their target changes are detected as divergence and hold supported publication; their writes are not prevented by this protocol. Operator-only `adopt-target` records the exact diverged SHA, invalidates prior admission evidence and leaves recovery held. Operator-only `force` records an unauthenticated claim that prior hosts and credentials are stopped or externally fenced; it cannot waive pending external operations or fresh provider checks. Git and provider APIs do not share a transaction, so uncertain requests remain reservations until their actual outcomes can be reconciled.
