## User thread

**Top-level:**

```text
*Live on production — User — Cassy v3.51.0*
Was: separate machines worked blind. → Now: Cassy shows who is working on your project and helps you avoid taking the same task.
```

**Reply (Was → Now):**

```text
*Working across machines*

• *See the other work* — Was: another copy of your project was out of sight. → Now: Cassy shows the other sessions, their machines, current work and recent activity, including a closer view of tasks and branches.

• *Current, visible connections* — Was: a failed connection could stay broken and the work shown could stay out of date. → Now: Cassy retries, refreshes the view and tells you when it cannot connect. Turning off cloud sharing keeps your session details on your machine.

• *Messages reach the other machine* — Was: a message could stay on your machine instead of reaching its destination. → Now: it goes through Cassy Cloud, with replies and delivery status you can check.

• *Clear send results* — Was: a message could promise later delivery to a name that did not exist. → Now: unknown names and destinations that cannot receive fail at once, connection errors explain the cause, and acceptance by the other mailbox is distinguished from confirmed delivery.

• *Avoid taking the same task* — Was: two machines could start the same task. → Now: a task held elsewhere on your account is refused before more work starts, with the holder and expiry shown. An explicit takeover is identified.

• *Agree on a division of work* — Was: dividing a larger job depended on remembering who took each part. → Now: both sides can agree on their shares, and Cassy refuses starts on the other side's parts until the agreement ends.

• *Know the account limit* — Was: an empty task list could look like no work was taken. → Now: Cassy says when another account's claims are hidden. Automatic claim protection is per account today. Across accounts, agree by message and record the division of work.

• *Receive after logging in* — Was: starting Cassy before login could leave incoming messages stuck until a restart. → Now: receiving starts after login without restarting, and the status view says whether messages are being received or why they are not.

• *Retries without extra copies* — Was: retrying a send could deliver the same message twice. → Now: a temporary failure is reported and kept for automatic retry for up to 15 minutes, with one delivery per send. Sending the same words again still counts as a new message.

• *One useful cleanup notice* — Was: a message that never arrived could disappear without warning. → Now: the sender gets its ID and reason, with one combined notice when several messages are cleared together.

*Smaller fixes*

• *Readable terminal output* — Was: connection details could run past the terminal edge and section rules could render badly in a basic text locale. → Now: lines fit or wrap, and the rules use plain ASCII where needed.

• *Safety settings stay yours* — Was: settings that relax safety checks could be changed from a working session. → Now: those changes require you at a terminal, and protected connections cannot be removed or replaced from a working session.

Install: `cas update` for an existing installation, or download the v3.51.0 archive for Linux x86_64 (SHA-256 `{{LINUX_SHA256}}`) or macOS ARM64 (SHA-256 `{{MACOS_SHA256}}`) from the GitHub release.
```

## Dev thread

**Top-level:**

```text
*Live on production — Dev — Cassy v3.51.0*
Was: repository sessions had local-only ownership. → Now: shared discovery, messaging and task claims connect them across machines.
```

**Reply (Was → Now):**

```text
*Repository peers and task ownership*

• *Project-scoped discovery* — Was: sessions had no view of repository peers. → Now: discovery uses `?project=<canonical_id>`, excludes locally registered sessions, honours stale status and lists machine, focus and heartbeat age.

• *Presence recovery* — Was: initial failure stayed failed and focus froze at startup. → Now: presence retries with bounded backoff, re-registers after 401/404, and heartbeats refresh project, focus, branch and hostname. Status opens with the local presence state. `cloud.auto_sync=false` disables publishing session details while discovery remains one-way.

• *Cloud mailbox routing* — Was: remote messages could remain in the local queue. → Now: name or name@machine routes through the peer mailbox, `in_reply_to` replies to the sender and `message_status` reads delivery. Peer messages carry no operator authority and cannot be urgent.

• *Honest send failures* — Was: unknown targets could return a misleading pending receipt. → Now: only pending local starts qualify for that fallback, unknown names and peers without a receiving mailbox fail immediately, and cloud refusals preserve the recipient and cause. Status 404 says the message is not visible to this account or does not exist.

• *Task claims* — Was: local leases allowed duplicate starts across machines. → Now: starts and claims acquire a repository-scoped cloud claim, renew it while held and release it on terminal or ownership transitions. A conflict preserves the holder and reports expiry. Cloud outages explicitly fall back to the local lease with peers unchecked.

• *Refuse before starting more work* — Was: a peer-held assignment could queue new work before warning. → Now: the peer check refuses it before queueing unless forced. Clearing or moving focus releases its claim, and losing a claim reports the loss instead of silently continuing.

• *Detailed peer view* — Was: only startup focus was visible. → Now: `cas peers --detail` and `--peer` show claims, live work and branches in bounded-width groups. JSON preserves structured details, split state and separate mailbox health.

• *Per-account claim boundary* — Was: hidden claims could look like an idle peer. → Now: a different account is labelled as having claims not visible. Claims are still per account, while discovery and messaging span team members. Team-wide claims remain open in petra-stella-cloud#155: https://github.com/Richards-LLC/petra-stella-cloud/issues/155

• *Explicit task partitions* — Was: no durable agreement divided a larger task's children. → Now: `epic_split` proposes shares, claims only the caller's share and accepts, rejects, cancels or expires the agreement. Local start and spawn checks enforce the other side's share even offline. Cancellation releases only the caller's claims.

*Delivery and smaller fixes*

• *Inbound recovery and health* — Was: startup before login disabled receiving until restart and receive errors went quiet. → Now: receiving recovers after login, retries failures with backoff and exposes receiving or the current cause in peers and both status views.

• *Durable logical sends* — Was: retry identity could change and duplicate delivery. → Now: each persisted send has one stable key, automatic retries with leases and backoff, a 15-minute TTL, and pending/retrying/sent/abandoned status. Identical deliberate sends get separate rows. Missing-token and 401 failures are not queued for retry.

• *Abandonment notices* — Was: expired delivery could disappear silently or generate a burst of notices during cleanup. → Now: abandonment notifies the sender atomically, and bulk sweeps aggregate per sender. A notice survives a removed sender through a fallback destination. GH #1186.

• *Terminal fit* — Was: presence lines exceeded 80 columns and `cas status --verbose` always used Unicode rules. → Now: peer rows wrap, details fit 72 columns, and `LC_ALL=C` uses ASCII rules.

• *Restart regression fixture* — Was: force restart raced the test's recordless/stopping precondition. → Now: SIGTERM and the existing barrier establish that state before force restart, preserving the assertions. Thirty consecutive loaded runs passed. Test-only, with no runtime behavior change.

• *Operator-only safety controls* — Was: guard-relaxing config and protected MCP connections could be changed from a working session. → Now: config changes require a terminal operator, hooks refuse direct config edits and guarded shell commands, and CLI/MCP protect restricted connections. Ordinary settings and project write roots retain their existing rules.

Published archives: `cas-x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu.tar.gz` (SHA-256 `{{LINUX_SHA256}}`) and `cas-aarch64-apple-darwin.tar.gz` (SHA-256 `{{MACOS_SHA256}}`), both built by CI regardless of the tagger's host.
```
