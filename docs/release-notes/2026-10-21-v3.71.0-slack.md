## User thread

Top-level:

```text
*Live on production — User — Cassy v3.71.0*
Was: routine updates could lose evidence or stall on stale checks. → Now: checks follow your changes and recovery is clearer.
```

Reply:

```text
*QA checks describe what good looks like*

• *Focused re-checks* — Was: a shared change could miss a part of the product that needed checking.
→ Now: checks follow the affected journeys, including changes shared by several journeys.

• *Checks with an owner* — Was: a check that had not been written could stay on the list without a deadline.
→ Now: unfinished checks name an owner and due date, and overdue checks are flagged.

• *Clear examples* — Was: different people could judge the same example differently.
→ Now: checks can define their meaning and require a label on each example, with missing labels shown as gaps.

• *Measurable limits* — Was: a numeric check could not say how much change was acceptable.
→ Now: check definitions can state an exact result or an acceptable change from an earlier measurement.

*QA warnings and evidence stay trustworthy*

• *Visible check gaps* — Was: unfinished checks were hard to distinguish or compare over time.
→ Now: status names the kinds of gaps and shows how counts changed since the previous check.

• *Reliable safeguards* — Was: a change could accidentally weaken the checks meant to review it.
→ Now: existing safeguards remain in force and a failed check calculation cannot silently remove a known problem.

• *Readable check errors* — Was: one malformed comparison could hide the rest of the check list.
→ Now: the bad entry is named while the remaining checks stay readable.

• *Complete comparisons* — Was: a before-and-after review could lose its before image or demand a comparison that was never requested.
→ Now: requested comparisons retain their before images, and optional comparisons stay optional.

• *Readable reports* — Was: a report label could appear apart from its heading.
→ Now: each label stays with the heading it describes.

*Inspect documentation drift before enforcing it*

• *Warnings before enforcement* — Was: outdated project guidance could become a surprise when stricter checks were switched on.
→ Now: status shows what is out of date, and switching on enforcement warns about known gaps first.

*Documentation follows the active checkout*

• *The right project guidance* — Was: freshness warnings could refer to another checkout or to queued work.
→ Now: warnings describe the work currently open and explain how to bring it up to date.

• *Overview coverage* — Was: the product overview could quietly stop tracking the folders that mattered.
→ Now: its watched folders are declared and a missing coverage scope produces a warning.

*Work resumes and hands over without losing its history*

• *Resume your work* — Was: returning from review could reject changes that already belonged to the work in progress.
→ Now: the existing work is recognized across review pauses and updates, while unrelated edits remain protected.

• *Keep work recognizable* — Was: bringing two sets of edits together could make your own change look unrelated.
→ Now: Cassy still recognizes your change after you resolve the conflict, so handover can continue.

• *Stable change receipts* — Was: later updates could change what an earlier handover receipt appeared to cover.
→ Now: each receipt stays tied to the change it originally recorded.

• *Current check results* — Was: an old success notice could appear to approve a newer change.
→ Now: check notices are revalidated against the exact change they describe.

• *Trustworthy proof* — Was: a partial note could be mistaken for proof of a different change.
→ Now: proof keeps its full origin and must match the complete recorded note.

• *Safe settings changes* — Was: changing settings from an internal data folder could alter machine settings, and reset saved a fixed default.
→ Now: settings edits there report that no project is initialized. Reset removes the saved setting so the normal default applies.

• *Review repairs first* — Was: an automatic repair could be recorded before you reviewed it.
→ Now: on shared branches, the repair is left for you to review before it is recorded.

*Hub commands and services explain their outcomes*

• *Clear restart results* — Was: a restart could give conflicting machine-readable results or lose the successful stop step when starting failed.
→ Now: one result records what stopped and what failed to start.

• *A useful connection remedy* — Was: a connection refusal could leave you without a usable next step.
→ Now: the cause and the supported retry choices appear together.

• *Readable service status* — Was: service status could wrap badly and leave the repair step unclear.
→ Now: compact status rows put a repair command beneath the result and explain failed readiness checks.

*Configuration edits preserve the surrounding file*

• *Small settings edits* — Was: changing one setting could rewrite unrelated settings and formatting.
→ Now: only the named setting changes, including supported aliases and inline tables.

*Concurrent changes publish safely*

• *Concurrent updates* — Was: two simultaneous updates could leave one change saved only locally while a retry claimed it was complete.
→ Now: publication checks the remote result and safely retries a competing update once.

• *Useful launch errors* — Was: startup errors could lose their cause or point at a log that did not exist.
→ Now: errors retain the cause and point at the actual diagnostic log.

*Disk space recovers before builds are refused*

• *Disk space recovery* — Was: old build files could fill the disk and stop new work until someone cleaned up.
→ Now: unused managed build files are reclaimed automatically, while active files, recent caches and unrelated folders stay protected.

*Account checks keep their real cause*

• *Accurate sign-in diagnosis* — Was: a locked Mac keychain during an SSH session could look like a signed-out account.
→ Now: the message identifies the keychain problem and explains how to unlock it locally.

• *Honest sign-in status* — Was: a broken cache could hide whether you were signed in.
→ Now: the real sign-in check still runs and its result stays visible.

*Release tooling uses verified inputs*

• *Fewer interrupted releases* — Was: a brief failure to find a prepared release download could stop the release.
→ Now: the lookup retries within its time limit and retains a useful failure cause if it still fails.

Install: use `cas update` after publication.
```

## Dev thread

Top-level:

```text
*Live on production — Dev — Cassy v3.71.0*
Was: stale refs and cached evidence could mislead delivery checks. → Now: immutable proof, guarded cleanup and registry validation follow the actual change.
```

Reply:

```text
*QA checks describe what good looks like*

• *Selected journeys* — Was: QA could depend on a legacy catalog or miss a shared pipeline change.
→ Now: Commander uses its tracked journey registry and semantic path selection, including shared-pipeline fan-out, while preserving catalog parity.

• *Pending checkers* — Was: an unwritten automated check had no accountable owner or deadline.
→ Now: criteria can name a pending checker with an owner and due date. Overdue checkers fail validation, and manual checks distinguish human judgment from automation still to be written.

• *Labeled criteria* — Was: example fixtures had no shared labels for judging a criterion.
→ Now: criteria can declare a definition and require per-fixture labels. Missing labels become debt and unknown or contradictory labels are rejected.

• *Numeric comparisons* — Was: numeric criteria could not declare exact equality or an allowed change from a baseline.
→ Now: criteria accept exact or banded comparisons with a minimum ratio or maximum delta. Invalid bands and misspelled or contradictory criterion fields are diagnosed.

*QA warnings and evidence stay trustworthy*

• *Debt and history* — Was: QA status hid types of unfinished checks and had no previous count for comparison.
→ Now: status separates curation, manual-check, pending-checker and unlabeled-fixture debt, retaining previous check counts to show the change.

• *Enforcement cannot weaken itself* — Was: a delivery could relax its own upkeep policy, or impact errors could erase an established refusal.
→ Now: the stricter base or delivered policy remains authoritative, proven registry findings survive impact failures, and malformed base policy sets a conservative floor without upgrading a warning-only delivered policy.

• *Malformed comparisons* — Was: one malformed numeric comparison could make the whole journey registry unreadable.
→ Now: validation reports the bad comparison as one actionable registry problem so the remaining entries can still be inspected.

• *Before-and-after evidence* — Was: declared visual comparisons could drop before-capture cells, while optional pair rules could apply even when no comparison was declared.
→ Now: declared before cells must survive comparison planning, optional coverage is enforced only when declared, shared pairs and diagnostic findings are preserved, and recorded waivers can recover.

• *Report section labels* — Was: release-report labels could separate from their headings.
→ Now: labels stay with their section headings in report layouts.

*Inspect documentation drift before enforcing it*

• *Upkeep status and warnings* — Was: turning on enforcement could reveal documentation drift only after it started blocking work.
→ Now: `cas qa journeys --status` reports registry, CODEMAP and overview drift against a pinned integration commit. A real transition to enforcement warns about known positive drift after saving the setting, while zero and unknown remain distinct.

*Documentation follows the active checkout*

• *Current documentation reference* — Was: freshness checks could inspect the main checkout, a queued assignment or the wrong integration branch.
→ Now: checks use the current checkout and active task target, preserve existing ledger stores, show the measured reference and update time, and name the target-sync command when a stale checkout blocks editing.

• *Overview watch paths* — Was: overview upkeep could fall back to nonexistent default directories and watch nothing silently.
→ Now: watch paths come from tracked overview declarations or configured existing paths, with an explicit warning when no product scope can be determined.

*Work resumes and hands over without losing its history*

• *Resume after review* — Was: resuming a review hold could reject the same task’s own accepted commits.
→ Now: owned delivery commits remain attributable across review holds, anchored target merges and dirty edits. A fresh task still refuses unrelated uncommitted work.

• *Rebased delivery ownership* — Was: rebasing after a conflict could make a valid delivery look unrelated or let a foreign subject pass through a body reference.
→ Now: fresh parking binds to the task-owned rebased delivery and checks the commit subject independently of references in its body.

• *Squash receipt range* — Was: later documentation merges could distort the change range proved by a squash receipt.
→ Now: a first-parent squash receipt is measured against its own parent instead of a later target position.

• *Released CI evidence* — Was: a queued success notice could outlive the exact delivery it had checked.
→ Now: release revalidates the immutable delivery and current CI evidence, marks superseded signals explicitly, and preserves relabeled prompts through native and batch handoff.

• *Proof provenance* — Was: trusted notes could be partially matched, truncated or reused outside their original provenance block.
→ Now: whole provenance blocks are preserved and trusted notes match exactly from the start of a complete note.

• *Store discovery and config reset* — Was: discovery inside a `.cas` folder could adopt its enclosing store, and reset saved a default override.
→ Now: discovery stops at `.cas`, so `cas config set` there reports not initialized. `cas config reset <key>` removes the stored key instead of writing a default.

• *Staged managed repairs* — Was: reconciling managed files could create an unexpected commit on an integration branch.
→ Now: integration-branch repairs remain staged for review, using the full HEAD reference to classify the branch.

*Hub commands and services explain their outcomes*

• *Restart JSON receipt* — Was: `cas hub restart --json` could emit more than one value or lose the completed stop step when starting failed.
→ Now: restart emits one JSON receipt, preserves stop evidence across start failures and raced stops, and fits its help text to terminal width.

• *Transport refusal remedy* — Was: required-transport errors could omit the explicit loopback remedy or obscure the connection cause.
→ Now: the refusal names the cause and the supported loopback or socket-selection retry.

• *Service readiness* — Was: service status could wrap badly or omit a repair action, probe failure cause or manual reboot remedy.
→ Now: readiness rows and custom profile labels fit narrow terminals, the repair command sits beneath the verdict, and failed probes retain their causes and recovery guidance.

*Configuration edits preserve the surrounding file*

• *In-place config changes* — Was: `cas config set` could rewrite the whole TOML document, reorder tables and materialize defaults.
→ Now: only the named value is edited in place, including supported aliases, dotted keys and inline tables, while unrelated formatting and values stay intact.

*Concurrent changes publish safely*

• *Concurrent merge publication* — Was: a peer advancing origin between fetch and push could leave a local-only merge and a retry could falsely say already contained.
→ Now: per-target locking covers merge paths, containment uses a fresh explicit origin ref, and an ordinary non-fast-forward push gets one fetch/merge/non-force retry without widening transactional review pins.

• *Safe spawn references* — Was: a divergent or unverifiable spawn base could be accepted or a refusal could name the wrong checkout.
→ Now: configured origin evidence is verified, unsafe divergence is refused, and diagnostics name the unpublished branch and actual checkout.

• *Launch diagnostics* — Was: preflight causes and runtime-log locations could be lost, or speculative log paths could appear before a launch decision.
→ Now: refusals retain their causes and actual log pointers, announce logs only after the decision, and keep pre-initialization logs in the host directory.

*Disk space recovers before builds are refused*

• *Managed scratch cleanup* — Was: relocated build targets were omitted from cache cleanup and a full disk required manual intervention.
→ Now: GC inventories verified scratch targets on the admission filesystem, preserves live and warm targets, and admission attempts guarded cleanup once before remeasuring free space. Safe shutdown and replacement retire their target after preserving proof.

*Account checks keep their real cause*

• *SSH keychain diagnostics* — Was: a locked macOS login keychain over SSH could look like a signed-out Claude account.
→ Now: checks identify the unavailable keychain and provide a local unlock remedy. Repeated account probes reuse bounded evidence without refreshing its age.

• *Optional auth cache failure* — Was: a broken optional cache could hide the account’s actual login state.
→ Now: cache failures fall back to the real authentication probe and preserve its evidence, including a genuine logout.

*Release tooling uses verified inputs*

• *Exact-revision QA candidate* — Was: building a QA binary required hand-written scratch commands and could miss Zig, use an unverified revision or grow a shared target without bound.
→ Now: `cas qa candidate build --task <id> --rev <full-sha>` resolves the release environment, checks binary version and revision, publishes the canonical manifest, and removes its private bounded target after artifact publication.

• *Prebuild lookup retries* — Was: a transient artifact lookup failure could abort an otherwise valid release wait.
→ Now: prebuild lookups retry within the wait bound and preserve a sanitized failure cause if the lookup still cannot complete.

• *Release help width* — Was: release-report help could run beyond a normal terminal width.
→ Now: report help wraps to the terminal width without dropping the latency options.

*Internal*

• *Codex command workdir* — Was: command admission could interpret a Codex exec call in the wrong directory or lose the source of a redirected write.
→ Now: the native hook reads the explicit workdir, retains each write’s source and preserves safe relative-log admission while explaining refused omitted-workdir redirects.

• *Split-state reconciliation* — Was: old shipped split bindings or repair/cleanup passes could strand or overwrite surviving child bindings.
→ Now: stale shipped splits stop binding children and cloud repair/cleanup preserve the surviving split state.

• *Portable verifier artifacts* — Was: verifier permissions differed across supported instruction flavors.
→ Now: all shipped verifier flavors can fetch the task artifacts needed to inspect portable proof.

• *Project maps and published records* — Was: the carried-forward project map, overview and release artifacts did not reflect the assembled changes.
→ Now: the structural map and product overview are refreshed, the previous release report and publication receipts are retained, and shared builtin reference history stays synchronized.

```
