## User thread

Top-level:

```text
*Live on production — User — Cassy v3.87.0*
Was: Commander lost connections and buried the work. → Now: it reconnects steadily and makes conversations and progress easier to follow.
```

Reply (Was → Now):

```text
*Stay connected and know what happened*

• *Steadier connections* — Was: Commander could flicker between connected and disconnected or stay waiting after your machine recovered.
→ Now: Slow background reads do not hold up connection checks, and recovery retries stay bounded.

• *Honest status* — Was: A machine starting up could look broken, and refused access could leave Working on.
→ Now: Startup says Connecting; lost or refused access clears Working.

• *A clear ending* — Was: Leaving with /exit could leave a stale work panel.
→ Now: A dated Session ended receipt tells you what stopped and what is still running.

• *Your messages stay yours* — Was: Terminal-typed messages could appear late, and a message already received could still say Not confirmed.
→ Now: Terminal input appears in order, and sent messages retain their own identity through reload and replay.

*Find the work and read it comfortably*

• *Project navigation* — Was: Sessions were a flat list with little context.
→ Now: Project cards, Needs you, machine filters and reported account details help you find the right conversation.

• *Release progress* — Was: A narrow panel dumped the whole task board beside unavailable buttons.
→ Now: Read merged progress, current work and tasks that need you. All tasks opens the wider board; permission guidance folds into one line.

• *Room to read* — Was: The progress panel stayed narrow and phone feedback could cover a task.
→ Now: Resize by dragging or keyboard, keep your chosen width, and read phone feedback without overlaps. Scrollbars match your theme.

• *Readable replies* — Was: Table words broke into single letters and headings and warnings blended together.
→ Now: Tables wrap whole words and formatting, headings keep their levels, and notes and warnings stand out.

• *Code and files* — Was: Copying code meant selecting it, previews sat apart from files and sent pictures became paths.
→ Now: Code has Copy and Wrap. Files stay with their previews; your sent pictures remain thumbnails after reload, including on another device.

• *Preview recovery* — Was: A failed picture could leave an empty panel and stay failed.
→ Now: Failures are compact and retry after reconnect.

*Accounts, Macs and updates*

• *Account choices* — Was: The picker made capacity hard to compare and could lose text typed ahead.
→ Now: Weekly usage leads each usable account's row, recommendations are clear, and typed answers keep their normal meaning.

• *Claude and Mac support* — Was: Claude usage came from a direct private request, and valid Mac logins could be refused.
→ Now: Usage comes from Claude's own tool, Mac credentials are recognized consistently and startup handoffs stay compact.

• *The hosted app stays current* — Was: A runtime release did not update hosted Commander.
→ Now: Publication deploys its released Commander bundle automatically.

Update with `cas update`, or choose <https://github.com/Richards-LLC/cassy/releases/tag/v3.87.0|the release downloads> for Linux x86_64 or macOS ARM64.
```

## Dev thread

Top-level:

```text
*Live on production — Dev — Cassy v3.87.0*
Was: hub authentication and catalog load could amplify recovery traffic. → Now: cached identity reads and isolated health checks keep recovery bounded.
```

Reply (Was → Now):

```text
*Hub and Commander*

• *Recovery* — Was: Slow subscription and catalog work blocked health checks and recovery churn shared retry pressure.
→ Now: Health and interactive service are isolated, sessions retain independent backoff and outage recovery is bounded.

• *Measured recovery* — Was: A dropped connection could leave stale state waiting.
→ Now: The measured check detects the drop in about 2 seconds and recovers in about 4 seconds; this is a measured check, not a network-wide guarantee.

• *Auth persistence* — Was: Requests repeatedly parsed and rewrote accumulated connection tickets and synchronously appended audit data.
→ Now: Ticket pruning, cached identity reads and batched rotating audit writes reduce contention while retaining revocation checks and capability compatibility.

• *Measured store size* — Was: 7.96 MB. → Now: 88 KB in the recorded comparison.

• *Measured lock-wait p95* — Was: 504 ms. → Now: 0.07 ms in that comparison.

• *Session truth* — Was: Ended identity vanished during teardown, terminal capture could lag completion and durable sends lost their client reference.
→ Now: Generation-bound exit receipts survive cleanup, terminal input orders by input time and durable history correlates the original client reference before text.

• *Scoped progress* — Was: The project-wide epic catalog was treated as session ownership.
→ Now: Header and task groups share an open, pinned-first or owner-based filter, matching names and registration IDs. Optional verification ownership is carried on the wire; All tasks remains explicit.

• *Rich chat* — Was: Prose tables collapsed columns, split messages damaged fenced code and status folding could consume code-shaped replies.
→ Now: Word-aware tables render inline formatting, code frames preserve source with Copy and Wrap, and headings and callouts keep their semantics.

• *Attachments and work detail* — Was: Preview failures stayed cached and sent uploads exposed host paths; background command output could disappear.
→ Now: Session-bound previews recover on reconnect, sent uploads hydrate across devices and typed work facts retain background-command outcomes with legacy fallback.

*Runtime and delivery correctness*

• *Official Claude usage* — Was: Subscription credentials were used for a direct private usage request.
→ Now: Capacity is consumed only from the official binary's native statusline output.

• *Account input and portability* — Was: Picker buffers crossed prompt boundaries, profile spelling affected Keychain lookup and push checks used GNU-only commands.
→ Now: Buffered input retains terminal editing and EOF semantics, profile paths normalize and readable Mac credentials work, and distribution checks are portable.

• *Startup and help* — Was: Codex startup emitted raw context or invalid hook output; help could require a default Claude login and empty Codex homes propagated.
→ Now: Native startup produces compact valid handoffs, help parses first and empty homes are unset.

• *Close and review identity* — Was: Accumulated branch changes, stale tips or competing proof pointers could misclassify a delivery.
→ Now: Actual attributed commits and explicit final receipts govern classification; independent evidence, renamed paths, accepted drops and identical-tree repairs keep their identities.

• *Notifications and state* — Was: Background review dispatch could expire silently, later transitions could swallow completion relays and routine reads repeated settled bodies.
→ Now: Dispatch is delivered, durable relays survive transitions, delayed notices are labelled and recipient projections retain intent without replaying settled content.

• *Artifacts and diagnostics* — Was: Peer artifact retrieval depended on local lookup, and partial issue queries or schema errors could yield misleading doctor results.
→ Now: Visible Cloud metadata resolves committed evidence, authenticated metadata reads refuse redirects, and diagnostics retain partial results with explicit unavailable states.

• *Local safety and cleanup* — Was: Literal clipboard text could trip policy guards, expanding heredocs escaped inspection and retired-cache scans depended on a global budget.
→ Now: Literal and expanding bodies are distinguished, missing bodies fail closed, memory writes stay bound to the active profile/project and known retired caches can be reclaimed while preserving proof.

• *Process ownership and checks* — Was: Cleanup could miss child processes, resumed sessions lost server ownership and push checks could inspect the wrong checkout.
→ Now: Process-tree cleanup and resumed ownership are retained; checks bind to the pushed branch and accept named CommonJS browser cases.

• *Hosted deployment* — Was: Runtime publication left the hosted web build behind and transient promotion checks could roll back a healthy deployment.
→ Now: Publication deploys its released bundle, pins the deployment CLI, removes the GitHub token from its environment and retries the live read before rollback.

```
