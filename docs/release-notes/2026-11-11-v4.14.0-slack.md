## User thread

Top-level:

```text
*Live on production — User — Cassy v4.14.0*
Was: shipped work sat open after every release. → Now: publishing closes everything it shipped, once.
```

Reply (Was → Now):

```text
*Releases that clean up after themselves*

• *Releases close their own work* — Was: after a release, shipped tasks and workstreams stayed open and needed hours of manual cleanup.
→ Now: publishing a Cassy release, or promoting a project from staging to main, closes everything it shipped exactly once, and sends one notice listing anything left, with its owner and next step.

• *Fixed issues close too* — Was: GitHub issues stayed open after their fix shipped.
→ Now: an issue closes when every fix that references it has shipped, with a comment naming the version.

• *Only real questions wait on you* — Was: Tasks & progress and Team mixed genuine questions with work already moving, and finished workstreams stayed in the list.
→ Now: only open questions ask for your answer, and finished workstreams leave the list.

• *Quality at a glance* — Was: no single view showed whether things were getting better or worse.
→ Now: Quality, from the command palette, leads with the measure that slipped most since the last release, with its trend.

• *Names open timelines* — Was: a separate Activity pill opened a timeline, and the card and timeline could disagree on state.
→ Now: click a name on its card to open its timeline, and both show the same state.

• *Slack shows progress on your request* — Was: a Slack request gave no sign it was picked up or finished.
→ Now: it gets eyes when acknowledged, a pick while work runs, and a green check once everything has shipped.
```

## Dev thread

Top-level:

```text
*Live on production — Dev — Cassy v4.14.0*
Was: release closeout was five manual steps per delivery. → Now: one shared reconciliation runs once per release.
```

Reply (Was → Now):

```text
• *Close at release* — Was: each shipped delivery needed a manual close, and the train closed directly at install.
→ Now: one reconciliation per release, keyed by {project, release, final SHA} in SQLite, closes every shipped delivery once; installed and backfill are recovery only.

• *Release inventory and evidence* — Was: membership and proof were rebuilt by hand at each close.
→ Now: the inventory binds the shipped SHA to every nested delivery and its proof, and close evidence is checked at the shipped commit.

• *Review sees the shipped bundle* — Was: pre-merge candidates kept the target's stale Commander bundle, so correct web deliveries were rejected.
→ Now: candidates regenerate committed output with the same script real merges use.

• *Fewer shared-file conflicts* — Was: parallel journey-registry edits collided on every merge.
→ Now: merges keep disjoint registry entries by ID, and shared-file notices tell workers how to proceed without supervisor relay.

• *Nested-shell commit guard* — Was: wrapping a commit in `bash -c '…'` slipped past the worker commit-scope guard.
→ Now: plain `-c` and `eval` are inspected with their effective directory, and other shell invocation forms fail closed.

• *Violet* — Was: uncertain posts could not always be reconciled, multi-part thread posts misreported success, and long managed threads lost mention authority.
→ Now: posting includes outcome reconciliation, thread posts confirm every part, and failed claims record a coded blocker for the owner.

• *Coordination* — Was: a requested worker answer could be capped like routine status, and day-old transcript copies raised delivery alarms.
→ Now: the first answer to an explicit ask wakes the supervisor, and only recent ask, blocker and answer copies alert, once per batch.

• *Factory fixes* — Build-overflow asks fit Cloud's limit, resumed queue work refreshes its branch base, derived-priority conflicts are refused with a reason, clean CODEMAP files stay switchable, and harness scratchpads can be created.

Validation: the merge queue proves the final release tree.
```
