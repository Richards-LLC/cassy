## What you can do now

### Messages that get through

#### Did it arrive?

Was: a sent message could leave you guessing.

Now: see RECEIVED when it reaches the recipient, or PENDING while Cassy keeps trying.

#### Honest delivery status

Was: a message that could not reach its reader could still look complete.

Now: see it as undelivered with a reason, and keep its content for the next person handling the conversation.

#### Peer handoff

Was: a message for someone who signed out could stall or be called stale.

Now: it reaches the next available person or explains why it could not.

#### Faster follow-up

Was: an idle conversation could wait two minutes for another nudge.

Now: Cassy checks after 30 seconds and tries again each minute.

#### Earlier alerts

Was: an unread message could wait two hours before an alert.

Now: the default alert tells you and the people responsible for your workspace after five minutes.

#### The right conversation

Was: a message could be picked up by a different conversation.

Now: it stays with the person it was meant for.

#### The next instruction

Was: an older closed request could hide a new assignment.

Now: each request stays clear, and an expired assignment gets an explanation.

#### Brief Slack outages

Was: a short connection loss could be handled differently by different Cassy actions.

Now: Cassy tries to reconnect briefly and gives the same clear answer if it cannot.

### Safer cleanup and recovery

#### Keep another project's work

Was: cleanup could miss a matching owner.

Now: it checks every relevant project and stops when ownership is uncertain.

#### Preview before signing in

Was: seeing which saved changes could move to your team required a login.

Now: preview locally without signing in; log in only to apply it.

#### A clearer next step

Was: saved updates waiting for a fix could hide a second problem or suggest a useless retry.

Now: see accurate counts, both findings and the right remedy.

#### Shared contact limits

Was: Cassy could miss a contact limit stored on another device.

Now: it checks that shared limit before sending and tells you when it cannot reach the other device.

#### Keep the whole note

Was: one invisible character could block a note's sync, and a workaround would delete it.

Now: the text stays unchanged and the note stays safely queued.

### Replies and setup

#### Files in Slack replies

Was: adding a file to an existing Slack reply needed manual setup.

Now: include the file when you send the reply.

#### Delivery handoff reports

Was: completion reports required manually assembling requirements and feedback rounds.

Now: a guided report tracks both in one branded handoff.

#### Quieter reminders

Was: prompts could repeat after action was taken or while a review was still underway.

Now: only reminders that still need action are sent.

#### Quicker Codex checks

Was: checking an installed version could add another wait.

Now: Cassy checks only when needed within its existing setup timeout.

#### Reliable updates

Was: an update could leave Cassy's hub on an older version or say it had stopped while it was still starting.

Now: Cassy gives a repair step and shows `starting` until ready.

#### Branded release reports

Was: release reports looked different from the rest of Cassy.

Now: the report and printout share Cassy's colors, lettering and logo, with clean page breaks.

## Under the hood

### Acknowledged delivery

#### Receipt verdict

Was: a pane handoff looked like completion.

Now: `message_status` leads with RECEIVED or PENDING from recipient evidence, and names ended deliveries without a receipt.

#### Abandoned messages

Was: an item could still report Delivered after wake checks were declined; the sender saw no cause and a successor lacked the message.

Now: it reports Undelivered, names the reason and carries content to the next live session.

#### Idle wake cadence

Was: the normal watchdog waited 120 seconds and retried once.

Now: it checks after 30 seconds, then nudges every 60 seconds up to five times while no turn has started.

#### Wake bursts

Was: several messages could repeat wake work, and an optional batch failure could interrupt delivery.

Now: directed wakes are debounced, share bounded deadlines and still deliver when batching fails.

#### Escalation

Was: unread messages could wait hours before escalation.

Now: priority and normal thresholds both default to 300 seconds, notifying the sender and operator.

#### Caller identity

Was: response fallback could drain a parent's mail for a child call.

Now: caller identity is checked before mail is surfaced or consumed.

#### Correct message context

Was: a message mentioning older work could be treated as a stale delivery.

Now: it is tied to the intended context, and a truly closed target gets an explicit refusal.

#### Violet reconnects

Was: command-line and background delivery classified proxy outages differently.

Now: both use the shared MCP reconnect mapping and bounded retry policy.

#### Peer succession

Was: messages for an ended or signed-out recipient could stall or look stale.

Now: they reach a verified successor when available, or return a clear refusal with bounded recovery details.

### Cloud and command safety

#### Cleanup ownership

Was: global task ownership and concurrent checkpoints could go unverified.

Now: project and global stores are checked read-only, with bounded retries and uncertain ownership refused.

#### Credential path resolution

Was: a command could reach saved credentials with relative paths after changing directories.

Now: directory changes and recursive path operands are checked before access.

#### Local queue preview

Was: a dry-run migration could trigger Cloud credential preflight.

Now: the read-only plan loads no credentials; applying it still requires authentication.

#### Queue diagnostics

Was: held personal rows counted even when they could not migrate, and generic retry advice obscured the cause.

Now: counts cover migratable rows and held-only queues point to the migration remedy.

#### Stable migration plans

Was: duplicate personal rows could make preview differ from apply.

Now: both use one deduplicated eligibility plan without discarding original rows.

#### Transient sync recovery

Was: a temporary network failure could leave personal sync work parked.

Now: bounded retries re-arm it without duplicating the queued work.

#### Lossless task notes

Was: NUL rejection advice blocked recovery and an interim repair stripped text.

Now: stored notes and queued payloads stay exact; rows remain parked until Cloud can round-trip NUL losslessly.

### Replies and review flow

#### Managed-thread files

Was: a local-file reply lacked automatic reply-intent fields and could be refused as external.

Now: the caller's intent supplies thread and claim checks for a normal file reply, with the same request id for a safe retry.

#### Replaced drafts

Was: a newer message from the same task could be reported as a dropped delivery after a sync changed its draft.

Now: its own replacement is recognized as a superseding update.

#### Actionable notifications

Was: already handled events or activity that still needed a review could trigger another prompt.

Now: notices follow current state and appear only when they still need action.

#### Cross-project proposals

Was: aliases could make an accepted task's target mismatch or leave ownership dependent on notes.

Now: registered project identity is resolved consistently and a local acceptance record grounds the receipt.

#### Accepted proposal history

Was: accepted work could disappear from its board or be pruned from untrusted notes.

Now: it stays visible and its acceptance record controls retention.

#### Delivery accounting

Was: shared or reassigned branches could make completed deliveries look stranded.

Now: close checks each delivery's own branch, including earlier owners' remaining commits.

#### Named task handoff

Was: a caller-supplied assignment tag or a busy session could start the wrong task.

Now: untrusted tags are discarded and each start binds to its named task.

#### Planning scope

Was: a plan could hide overlapping work or a local outage, or include work outside a peer's scope.

Now: warnings remain visible and plans stay within the requested scope.

#### Lifecycle-safe rework

Was: an older sync event could reopen work after a newer delivery decision.

Now: rework follows the latest task lifecycle action.

#### Merge alert completion

Was: a terminal fallback or delayed native alert could lose an unfinished merge notice.

Now: alert work is rechecked against the current inbox and completion is retained through fallback.

#### Generated skill history

Was: a close check could run an unsupported generator or compare stale generated references.

Now: it uses the supported scoped generator and rebuilds each output from merged history.

#### Task delivery range

Was: target-branch syncs could make a task's own delivery look like extra work.

Now: delivery checks skip target-sync merges and follow that task's branch tip.

#### Shared contact authority

Was: named-contact posts could proceed without a shared contact check, or leave the outcome unclear on timeout.

Now: authority is reserved before sending, retries keep one request identity and local uncertainty is visible.

### Proof, splits and setup

#### Portable proof

Was: evidence and its task context could stay on one machine or branch.

Now: synced notes carry proof artifacts, and parked reviews recover only for the owning machine and session.

#### ARTIFACT_PROOF field refusals

Was: selected evidence fields could accept caller-supplied markers.

Now: close/cancel/reopen/request_changes/QA reasons, transfer notes, stranded override, spawn_brief and worktree_merge reason refuse them.

#### Fair work splits

Was: a split needed both sides to request or grant at once, and expired claims could strand child work.

Now: either side can initiate, decisions are recorded, old children are retired before reuse, and a live successor can cancel an expired orphan after verifying its owner is gone.

#### Parked checkout adoption

Was: a receiving session could lose commits from a parked source checkout.

Now: adoption preserves its history and reconciles the linked checkout before work resumes.

#### Viktor opt-out

Was: a saved opt-out could be ignored by later prompts, and an explicit re-add could remain disabled.

Now: opt-out persists and an explicit add enables Viktor again.

### Developer and release tooling

#### Elegant tests

Was: test-first required a new file, and one verifier verdict could create a project rule.

Now: a changed test case in an existing file qualifies, while reusable lessons stay candidates until separate tasks repeat them.

#### Release reports

Was: empty issue captions could count outside the report ledger, and a closing folio could create a footer-only page.

Now: counts stay scoped to that ledger and the final page keeps its content.

#### Commander-branded reports

Was: report pages did not follow the Cassy hub's visual system.

Now: shared colors, type, mark, Was→Now cards and example captures carry through HTML and print.

#### Installed hub binary

Was: update could restart a stale launchd or systemd executable, with no repair guidance.

Now: it checks the service definition before restart and points to `cas hub service install` when they differ.

#### Startup readiness

Was: service install reported not running just after activation.

Now: it reports `starting` until the runtime is ready.

#### Hub status phases

Was: status could lose the startup phase or give drift advice outside the affected service.

Now: it retains the phase and scopes repair guidance to the detected drift.

#### Codex version probe

Was: suffixes and abbreviated versions confused model-cache checks.

Now: versions compare consistently, and an on-demand probe fits the existing five-second account deadline.

#### Linux isolation design

Was: changing HOME alone left absolute credential paths visible.

Now: the Linux prototype recommends a mount boundary and explicit tool/auth grants; production enforcement and macOS support remain pending.

#### SOW delivery reports

Was: completing client work relied on manually assembling requirements, feedback rounds and screenshots.

Now: a built-in workflow checks each outcome, tracks feedback, pairs requested changes with real captures and prepares a branded report and PM handoff.

#### SOW report hygiene

Was: ambiguous client terms or a sign-in redirect could pass as report evidence.

Now: client identifiers and standards are bounded, and login success is checked before capture.

#### Viktor import cleanup

Was: case and import variants could create duplicate or mismatched accounts.

Now: identities are normalized before cleanup.

#### Actionable login check

Was: a login preflight could overflow a narrow terminal.

Now: its explanation wraps and keeps the next action visible.

#### Readable SOW captures

Was: report evidence could be cramped on phones or lose full-width print flow.

Now: client identifiers are preserved and captures fit phone and print layouts.
