## What you can do now

### Commander

#### Phone sends

Was: A phone message could sit unseen in an idle conversation.

Now: Commander wakes the conversation, including for a short approval.

#### Brief busy spells

Was: A short busy spell could immediately show “Not sent”.

Now: Sending allows a two-second wait for the message to be saved.

#### Conversation handover

Was: An unread phone message could remain with a conversation that had ended.

Now: It moves once to the replacement conversation. Messages already seen stay in the original.

#### Two people, one hub

Was: Sending could be blocked by someone else’s input control, and names could disappear.

Now: Both people can send, leaving releases their control, and names stay with each message. Read-only access explains how to get permission.

#### Reconnect without reloading

Was: Replacing the running session or restarting the hub could leave a phone looking live but unable to receive.

Now: Commander reconnects and counts the open phone again without a page reload.

#### Replies kept safe

Was: An answer written during reconnect could be refused before it was saved.

Now: The answer is saved, held and shown when the conversation reconnects.

#### Unread on a new device

Was: A newly paired device could hide unread replies until you opened the conversation.

Now: Its list shows the unread count and latest reply preview straight away.

#### Honest delivery status

Was: A saved message could look delivered too early, or a replay could move its status backwards.

Now: Saved, delivered and replied stay distinct, and progress never rolls back.

#### Truthful activity

Was: An idle conversation could keep showing “working”.

Now: Working appears only while current activity is still awaiting an answer.

#### One-tap answers

Was: Offered answer buttons could disappear from a live reply or its history.

Now: The choices remain available in both.

#### Recovery notices

Was: A recovered connection could leave an outage warning, or a change could leave the list stale.

Now: The warning clears and the list refreshes even after an overlapping connection check fails.

#### Find a running project

Was: New session could omit a project that was already running.

Now: It includes those projects, respects folder-view permissions and checks names consistently.

### Access and diagnostics

#### Traceable connections

Was: A pairing or reconnection could leave an incomplete connection record.

Now: Successful pairing is recorded and reconnect records identify the machine.

#### Clearer send failures

Was: A refused send could leave little evidence of what happened.

Now: Its refusal is recorded at both ends for diagnosis.

#### Documentation completion

Was: A documentation change could fail its final check after its folder was reused.

Now: Its check stays tied to the reviewed version, and recovery can save the correction.

#### Report storage

Was: A custom evidence folder could be ignored when creating a release report.

Now: Reports use that folder and keep their evidence references portable.

#### Images ready to capture

Was: An image could still be blank when its screenshot passed.

Now: Visible images must finish decoding, and broken or timed-out images fail the check.

#### Evidence that stays connected

Was: Moving a capture or revising its notes could detach the recording or mislabel its origin.

Now: The recording stays linked to the original run and version.

#### Readable terminal lists

Was: Some terminal settings could display broken bullet symbols.

Now: Those terminals use readable plain-text bullets.

#### Downloads access

Was: Saving into Downloads could need extra setup in each project.

Now: Downloads is writable by default, and your saved extra folders apply across projects.

### Branded reports and captures

#### Complete comparisons

Was: A comparison could be missing one side or leave an unclear capture error.

Now: Configured captures require complete before-and-after pairs and explain what needs fixing.

#### Share a pairing code

Was: The person holding the phone had to relay its pairing instructions by hand.

Now: Send the code through the phone’s share sheet, or copy it, with approval steps and expiry included.

#### Branded comparisons

Was: Making a branded report comparison required separate capture steps.

Now: Capture fixed versions, check matching images and use the available branded report and customer-note commands.

#### Before and after in releases

Was: Release notes and reports could leave out an available comparison.

Now: They can include complete, checked before-and-after images with the change they explain.

### Session safeguards

#### Clean startup

Was: An invalid local connection path could leave a half-started session.

Now: Startup checks the path and stored ownership before creating the session.

#### Quieter review waits

Was: An existing review wait or unchanged result could trigger repeated alerts.

Now: The wait is recognized and unchanged results follow the normal retry interval.


## Under the hood

### Commander

#### Operator wake

Was: Verified phone input could be classified as quiet inbox traffic.

Now: Paired-device messages use operator wake priority, including approvals.

#### Store contention

Was: A send inherited an exhausted loop budget and failed immediately.

Now: Each send gets its own bounded two-second store wait.

#### Successor delivery

Was: Pending phone input could be stranded with an ended session.

Now: Verified unread operator rows move once to its successor, while seen rows remain.

#### Shared hub

Was: Message send depended on terminal input ownership and attribution could disappear.

Now: Send is independent of the input lease, departure releases owned control, sender names persist, and read-only Send explains its permission.

#### Replacement recovery

Was: Retired viewers and a refused subscription could strand an apparently live phone.

Now: Replacement retires old viewers, retries the catalog gap and restores the attached-device count after daemon or hub restart.

#### Durable replies

Was: A zero device count could reject an answer before persistence.

Now: The operator thread stores it first and reports held delivery until reattachment.

#### Unopened previews

Was: A fresh device did not hydrate unopened conversation tails.

Now: Initial history supplies unread state and previews before opening the thread.

#### Monotonic receipts

Was: Queue acceptance could imply execution, and history or receipt replay could regress progress.

Now: Durable acceptance is distinct from supervisor delivery, and delivered or replied states remain monotonic.

#### Working state

Was: An open connection could leave an idle supervisor marked as working.

Now: The indicator requires live unanswered activity.

#### Ask choices

Was: Answer choices were lost between CLI, live reply and history.

Now: The same options survive each path.

#### Recovery and catalog

Was: Recovered outages stayed in attention and a failed overlapping heartbeat could swallow a refresh.

Now: Recovery retires its attention item, and the fleet change still reads a fresh catalog.

#### Project discovery

Was: Live but unregistered projects were omitted and folder visibility was too broad.

Now: Live projects are included only with session-read access, and session names use shared CLI validation.

### Access and diagnostics

#### Connection audit

Was: Committed installation pairing lacked an audit row and reconnect rows lacked machine identity.

Now: Pairing writes its audit row and recovery records carry the hub machine ID.

#### Send telemetry

Was: Refused sends lacked correlated outcomes and history reads could look like sends.

Now: Daemon logs and hub outcomes record real sends, with bounded client references and no history-read send audit.

#### No-code proofs

Was: A reused checkout or unsupported store update could invalidate documentation completion or branch recovery.

Now: No-code proofs pin the reviewed revision and branch recovery uses the guarded store update.

#### Report artifacts

Was: Release reports assumed the default artifacts root.

Now: They read and name the configured artifacts root and use portable receipt references.

#### Decoded-image QA

Was: Capture could pass before visible images decoded, even with an exception configured.

Now: Decode precedes resting and journey screenshots, and failed images remain findings.

#### Journey bundles

Was: Nested output could inherit the wrong task identity and a documentation rebundle could lose a moved trace.

Now: Bundles retain their originating identity, executed revision and trace location.

#### C-locale output

Was: Styled bullets needed a visual exception in C locales.

Now: ASCII fallback keeps diagnostic output readable without the exception.

#### Write roots

Was: Downloads and user-wide folder grants were not applied consistently.

Now: The platform Downloads folder and user-level `factory.write_roots` join project roots through existing safety checks.

### Branded reports and captures

#### Capture requirements

Was: Configured capture could omit declared pairs and diagnostics were hard to read.

Now: Complete pairs are required, selector/mask/budget findings fit 80 columns, and reused dependencies remain clean.

#### Pairing share

Was: Pairing assumed the phone owner could use the machine’s terminal.

Now: Native sharing or clipboard fallback carries the code, approval command and expiry.

#### Immutable captures

Was: Report capture helpers were duplicated and comparisons lacked a shared integrity check.

Now: Journey and catalog capture use immutable revisions, optional masks and goal receipts, with validated image framing and integrity.

#### Release image pairs

Was: Notes and reports did not share verified comparison selection.

Now: Notes select JPG pairs from the released range and reports resolve Pair references. Missing or incomplete evidence adds no image.

### Session safeguards

#### Startup admission

Was: Invalid socket paths or store ownership could leave a partially registered daemon.

Now: Read-only admission refuses them before startup changes session state.

#### Wait and anchor checks

Was: A hold could be misclassified, settled results could re-alert, and anchor checks could miss the delivery branch.

Now: Full hold notes are classified, settled retries are respected and origin checks try the delivery branch first.
