## What you can do now

### Releases that clean up after themselves

#### Releases close their own work

Was: after a release, shipped tasks and workstreams stayed open and needed hours of manual cleanup.

Now: publishing a Cassy release, or promoting a project from staging to main, closes everything it shipped exactly once, and sends one notice listing anything left, with its owner and next step.

#### Fixed issues close too

Was: GitHub issues stayed open after their fix shipped.

Now: an issue closes when every fix that references it has shipped, with a comment naming the version.

#### Only real questions wait on you

Was: Tasks & progress and Team mixed genuine questions with work already moving, and finished workstreams stayed in the list.

Now: only open questions ask for your answer, and finished workstreams leave the list.

#### Quality at a glance

Was: no single view showed whether things were getting better or worse.

Now: Quality, from the command palette, leads with the measure that slipped most since the last release, with its trend.

#### Names open timelines

Was: a separate Activity pill opened a timeline, and the card and timeline could disagree on state.

Now: click a name on its card to open its timeline, and both show the same state.

#### Slack shows progress on your request

Was: a Slack request gave no sign it was picked up or finished.

Now: it gets eyes when acknowledged, a pick while work runs, and a green check once everything has shipped.


## Under the hood

### Release changes

#### Close at release

Was: each shipped delivery needed a manual close, and the train closed directly at install.

Now: one reconciliation per release, keyed by {project, release, final SHA} in SQLite, closes every shipped delivery once; installed and backfill are recovery only.

#### Release inventory and evidence

Was: membership and proof were rebuilt by hand at each close.

Now: the inventory binds the shipped SHA to every nested delivery and its proof, and close evidence is checked at the shipped commit.

#### Review sees the shipped bundle

Was: pre-merge candidates kept the target's stale Commander bundle, so correct web deliveries were rejected.

Now: candidates regenerate committed output with the same script real merges use.

#### Fewer shared-file conflicts

Was: parallel journey-registry edits collided on every merge.

Now: merges keep disjoint registry entries by ID, and shared-file notices tell workers how to proceed without supervisor relay.

#### Nested-shell commit guard

Was: wrapping a commit in `bash -c '…'` slipped past the worker commit-scope guard.

Now: plain `-c` and `eval` are inspected with their effective directory, and other shell invocation forms fail closed.

#### Violet

Was: uncertain posts could not always be reconciled, multi-part thread posts misreported success, and long managed threads lost mention authority.

Now: posting includes outcome reconciliation, thread posts confirm every part, and failed claims record a coded blocker for the owner.

#### Coordination

Was: a requested worker answer could be capped like routine status, and day-old transcript copies raised delivery alarms.

Now: the first answer to an explicit ask wakes the supervisor, and only recent ask, blocker and answer copies alert, once per batch.


#### Reliable work setup

Was: oversized overflow requests failed, new tasks could attach to a landed train, untouched resumed branches retained old bases, and clean navigation files blocked switching.

Now: requests fit the peer limit, new work joins the successor, resumed untouched branches refresh their base, and clean navigation files remain switchable.

#### Honest commands and delivery alerts

Was: derived-priority requests could silently keep the old value, check completions triggered redundant delivery alerts, and command assertions were mistaken for prose.

Now: conflicting priority requests fail atomically with a reason, delivered check completions stay quiet, and tests can assert executable redirects directly.

#### Measurable quality and scoped notes

Was: no per-supervisor release ledger exposed delivery counts and wake-source costs, and creating a permitted harness scratchpad could be refused.

Now: the read-only quality ledger reports merged deliveries, changed safeguards and turns by wake source; the current session can create its own scratchpad.
