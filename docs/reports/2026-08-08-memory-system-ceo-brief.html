Leadership decisions

  1. Confirm the operating position: retain local-first and legacy availability; do not authorize a “single source only” claim or legacy read-path removal on present evidence.
  2. Back the next milestone: prioritize changed-only delivery, project scoping, contamination-safe retrieval, and equivalent page treatment before broad cross-project/team use.
  3. Set the release bar: require a measured ranking-quality result, not only hit-count parity, before expanding reliance on the briefing.

What problem this solves

AI-assisted work repeatedly loses context: a new session or teammate has to rediscover what the project is, why decisions were made, and which constraints matter. Raw notes are noisy and costly to read in full. CAS keeps individual memories and creates concise, source-linked project briefings from durable material. A session gets an index and retrieves a full page only when needed.

This makes knowledge reusable without replacing the original record or requiring an always-on cloud service.

Privacy, safety, and governance posture

  • Local-first: no account means no cloud calls, cloud files, or semantic channel.
  • Human control: protected pages resist automated refresh, cleanup, and incoming synchronized overwrites.
  • Truthful capability reporting: absent semantic enrichment is reported as unavailable and weight goes to working local channels.
  • Sharing hardening: committed work targets scoping, foreign-project contamination defenses, and equivalent briefing-page behavior.

What is live today

The local system includes persistent memories, a project-briefing store, search, and deliberate distillation. Briefing pages are ordinary Markdown with a local index and source tracking. A session receives an index rather than full bodies; full pages are requested on demand. A hand-authored page can be locked, and the lock survives synchronization.

The migration closed with zero-loss accounting for mapped material: 146 pages are traced with zero orphans, and legacy entries were retained as permanent co-residents rather than deleted. A fresh read-only inventory now shows 2,256 live entries (1,615 project plus 641 global). These are implementation and inventory facts—not employee adoption, customer adoption, hours saved, revenue, or ROI measurements.

Known limits and operational risks
  1. Ranking quality is unmeasured. The completed test verifies results can be found, but its ten-result cap cannot establish that the best page appears first.
  2. Sharing and synchronization are unfinished. The active roadmap identifies snapshot-style delivery, scope contamination risk, and unequal page treatment as work to close. Global-page sharing representation is unresolved.
  3. Coexistence is intentional but complex. Keeping individual memories and pages protects continuity, yet makes “single source of truth” inaccurate today.
  4. No business-impact baseline. No production retrieval-success, time-saved, customer-outcome, financial-impact, or adoption measure was found. Forecasts are omitted rather than invented.
Already-committed roadmap

The active cloud-sync correctness program is the next committed delivery lane: changed-only and project-scoped delivery, foreign-project import prevention, honest flags, page-sharing parity or an explicit gate, and a global-page decision. This is operational hardening—not an ROI or adoption promise.

Retrieval must add a ranking-quality measurement on the same controlled corpus before the organization changes its replacement posture. The findability result is a meaningful gate passed, not final quality proof.
